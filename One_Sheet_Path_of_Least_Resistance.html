Behavior-Change Keynote
Path of Least Resistance

Stop trying to change people - change the path they are standing on.

The Talk

Why do smart, well-trained people skip the one step that would protect them? Brad spent a career on that question and landed somewhere uncomfortable: the problem is almost never the person. Using what forty years around electrical current taught him, he shows that people, like current, take the path of least resistance every time, and that no amount of training, posters, or pleading changes it.

The answer is not more motivation. It is design. Through stories from the oil sands to a Grade 8 classroom to a piece of software he built, he lays out a plain, repeatable framework for making the right thing the easy thing, so the behavior you want happens on its own, even when no one is watching.

What the Room Walks Away With

  • Information does not change behavior. Why the better memo and the longer training rarely land.
  • Print, Current, Path. A plain framework for why behavior does or does not happen.
  • Find the bright spot. The place it already works, and how to copy the path.
  • Shape the path, not the person. Make the right move the easy move.
  • Read the current. Measure what people actually do, not what they were told.
Where the whole talk started A sharp, certified, experienced hand skipped a ninety-second check he knew cold, and nearly lost everything for it. He was not lazy or careless. Understanding exactly why he skipped it is the key that unlocks the entire framework.

The Framework

PrintThe plan. Correct, important, and completely inert on its own.
CurrentWhat people actually do. It always runs downhill, toward the easy path.
PathThe terrain you design. Change it, and the behavior follows for free.
